Overview

A method for identifying amplitude-dependent nonlinear systems using Volterra kernels and Principal Component Analysis, and a dedicated FPGA architecture for real-time synthesis. The system targets audio applications where nonlinearity depends on the excitation level.

Problem

Many real-world audio systems — loudspeakers, amplifiers, transducers — exhibit nonlinear behavior that depends on the excitation amplitude. Classical linear identification fails to capture this. Full Volterra models are powerful but computationally prohibitive for real-time implementation.

Solution

Identification is performed from impulse responses captured at varying excitation levels. PCA is applied to collapse the trajectory of Volterra kernels into a compact set of coefficients, drastically reducing the computational cost. The resulting representation is implemented as a single pipelined convolution engine on FPGA.

Architecture

  • Identification: Volterra kernels from impulse responses at multiple excitation levels
  • Compression: PCA score collapse of kernel trajectories
  • Real-time hardware: FPGA-based parallelized, pipelined single-convolution engine
  • Validation platform: STM32 Black Pill + Xilinx Artix / Sipeed Tang FPGA
  • Application: real-time synthesis of amplitude-dependent nonlinear audio systems

My role

Full R&D: formulation of the identification method, PCA-based compression, hardware architecture design, RTL implementation, and hardware validation. Publication in progress.

Results

  • Single-convolution real-time synthesis on FPGA
  • Hardware validation in progress (STM32 + FPGA platform)
  • Publication in preparation
