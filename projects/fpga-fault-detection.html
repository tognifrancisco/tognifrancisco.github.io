Low-Latency Fault Detection for Power Inverters

FPGA-based open-switch diagnosis in electric drives

Published at CAE 2026 — G.E.A., UNRC

← Back to portfolio
Published · CAE 2026

Overview

An FPGA architecture for real-time open-switch fault detection in power semiconductor devices used in electric drive inverters. The design achieves detection latency as low as 10% of the electrical period, using only 5% of FPGA resources at a 100 MHz base clock.

Problem

Open-switch faults in inverter power semiconductors can cause degraded torque, overcurrent in healthy phases, and cascading failures if not detected quickly. Conventional software-based diagnosis runs on the main controller and introduces latency that may be too high for high-dynamic electric drives.

Solution

A dedicated FPGA architecture that executes the fault detection algorithm in hardware, parallel to the main control loop. The design is pipelined for minimum latency and integrated with the power stage for hardware validation.

Architecture

  • Target: Xilinx FPGA (Vivado flow)
  • Language: Verilog / HDL-RTL
  • Algorithm: real-time open-switch detection based on current signature analysis
  • Base clock: 100 MHz
  • Resource usage: ~5% of FPGA
  • Detection latency: 10% of electrical period (varies with drive operating frequency)

My role

R&D and FPGA implementation: algorithm study, RTL design, pipelining and optimization, timing closure, integration with the power electronics hardware, and validation. Co-author of the resulting publication.

Results

  • Detection latency: 10% of the electrical period
  • FPGA resource usage: 5% at 100 MHz base clock
  • Hardware-validated on a real electric drive
  • Published in Proc. 8th Argentine Conference on Electronics (CAE), UNT, 2026

Publication

F. Togni et al., "Low-Latency Architecture for Open-Circuit Fault Diagnosis in Electric Drive Inverter Power Switches," in Proc. 8th Argentine Conference on Electronics (CAE), UNT, 2026.
