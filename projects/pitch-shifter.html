Overview

Design and validation of two time-domain pitch shifting methods for embedded platforms. Both avoid the FFT and the phase management of a phase vocoder, targeting low latency and integer-only arithmetic on small microcontrollers.

Method 1 — Doppler-Based Pitch Shifter (2023)

A time-domain pitch shifter based on the Doppler effect, using two read pointers in a circular buffer that are crossfaded. A single variable delay line can't maintain a constant transposition — the read pointer eventually catches up to the write pointer. The two-pointer crossfade solves this: while one pointer fades out at the end of its admissible range, the other fades in.

The shift is correlated to musical intervals in both just intonation and equal temperament. The firmware was implemented in C on an ARM Cortex-M4F (Tiva C TM4C123G, TM4C123GH6PM at 80 MHz), running bare-metal with two hardware timers — one for ADC sampling, one for DAC update. End-to-end latency is 2 ms at 44.1 kHz. The algorithm works on polyphonic signals; known artifacts are transient duplication and detuning, both inherent to the Doppler time-domain approach.

  • Platform: TI TM4C123G (ARM Cortex-M4F at 80 MHz)
  • Language: C firmware, bare-metal
  • Sampling: 44.1 kHz
  • Latency: 2 ms end-to-end
  • Algorithm: Doppler effect with two crossfaded read pointers
  • Scales: just intonation and equal temperament
  • Analog front-end: 2nd-order multiple-feedback anti-aliasing filter (fc ≈ 20 kHz), 4-bit R2R DAC + comparator for successive-approximation ADC (12-bit → 16-bit effective resolution)
  • Analog output: 4-level differential PWM DAC (16 bits split as 2×8 bits), carrier 312.5 kHz from 80 MHz clock, 1:256 resistor weighting, RC low-pass
  • Interface: quadrature encoder with axial switch — full-resolution direction detection via single-line interrupt with edge toggle
  • Status: implemented and validated on hardware (TP_FF, 2023)

Method 2 — Weighted Granular Decimation of an Oversampled Buffer (2025)

A method that combines two ideas from different domains: oversampling with decimation for effective resolution gain (an ADC technique) and asynchronous full-buffer reading for pitch control (an audio technique). A circular buffer of length L = 4ⁿ is written at f_so = L·f_s and read as a whole at a synthesis rate f_sr = α·f_s. The ratio α = f_sr / f_s controls the pitch: α < 1 produces a higher pitch (samples are skipped), α > 1 produces a lower pitch (samples are repeated).

The output is a weighted sum of the entire buffer — not a single sample — so there is no phase discontinuity at block boundaries. The Hann window is scaled to a power of two so that normalization is a bit shift (L = 16, Q = 12, coefficients sum to 4096). Decimation is also a shift: sum L samples and shift right n bits, not divide by L. An analog pre-emphasis filter (first-order shelving) compensates for the sinc rolloff of the averaging filter, about 3.9 dB at Nyquist.

  • Method: weighted granular decimation of an oversampled buffer
  • Oversampling factor: L = 4ⁿ (L = 16, n = 2 bits resolution gain)
  • Window: Hann scaled to a power of two (Q = 12, Σ w̃ = 4096)
  • Decimation: sum + bit shift (not division)
  • Pre-emphasis: analog shelving filter, inverse of sinc response
  • Complexity: O(L) per output sample, ~48–64 cycles for L = 16
  • Validation: MATLAB simulation at 44.1 kHz
  • Planned implementation: STM32 BlackPill (ARM Cortex-M4F)
  • Status: preprint, simulated; hardware implementation planned

Why time-domain and not phase vocoder?

The phase vocoder gives good quality but introduces phasiness artifacts on polyphonic signals and costs O(N log N) per frame with FFT and iFFT. Both methods here are pure time-domain, O(L) per sample with small L, no FFT, no pitch estimation, no phase management. The tradeoff is a limited pitch range and, for the Doppler method, transient artifacts — which are documented honestly.

My role

R&D of both algorithms. For the Doppler shifter: full analog front-end design (anti-aliasing filter, 4-bit R2R DAC + comparator successive-approximation ADC, protection), C firmware on the Tiva C (two timers, encoder interface, PWM DAC output), and hardware characterization. For the decimation method: mathematical derivation (noise model, ENOB gain, decimation via bit shift, Hann scaling to power of two, pre-emphasis compensation), MATLAB simulation, and pseudocode for the two-timer implementation with double buffering.

Results

  • Doppler method: 2 ms end-to-end latency on Tiva C at 44.1 kHz, working on polyphonic signals
  • Doppler method: 4 bits of ADC resolution recovered via successive approximation
  • Decimation method: mathematical model complete, simulated in MATLAB at 44.1 kHz
  • Decimation method: expected ENOB gain of 2 bits (12 → 14), CPU load under 5% at 44.1 kHz on a 100 MHz Cortex-M4
  • Decimation method: preprint available
