Overview

A real-time pitch shifter implemented on an STM32 Black Pill microcontroller, correlated to musical intervals in both just and equal temperament scales. The system achieves 2 ms end-to-end latency at 44.1 kHz sampling.

Problem

Real-time pitch shifting usually involves trade-offs between latency, quality, and computational cost. Achieving low latency on a microcontroller (rather than a dedicated DSP or FPGA) requires an efficient algorithm and careful firmware optimization.

Solution

An adaptive decimation algorithm that shifts the pitch in musical intervals rather than arbitrary frequency ratios. This allows the pitch shift to remain musically coherent across the scale (both just and equal temperament), while keeping the computational cost low enough to run in real time on an ARM Cortex-M4F.

Architecture

  • Platform: STM32 Black Pill (ARM Cortex-M4F)
  • Language: C firmware
  • Sampling rate: 44.1 kHz
  • End-to-end latency: 2 ms
  • Algorithm: adaptive decimation correlated to musical intervals
  • Scales: just intonation and equal temperament

My role

R&D of the pitch shifting algorithm, C firmware implementation, optimization for real-time operation on ARM Cortex-M4F, and latency characterization.

Results

  • End-to-end latency: 2 ms
  • 44.1 kHz sampling on ARM Cortex-M4F
  • Musically coherent shifting in just and equal temperament
  • Real-time operation on low-cost MCU (no dedicated DSP)
